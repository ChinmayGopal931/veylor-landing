How it works

A split key and an atomic swap.

Your ZEC never leaves the shielded pool, your dollars land straight in Railgun, and at no point does anyone, including us, hold your money.

01 The split key

Your ZEC goes to a fresh shielded Zcash address that nobody can spend alone. Its spend key is two halves added together. The maker keeps half e, and you keep half z, made fresh for every swap from your seed. Spending needs e + z.

The Ethereum contract stores only the public points E = e·G and Z = z·G, on the Pallas curve, so it can check a half when one is revealed.

The only way to take money out of the contract is to reveal your half of the key, and revealing it hands the other side what it needs to take the ZEC.

02 A swap, ZEC to 0zkUSDC

  1. You and the maker

    Quote and accept

    The maker offers a price and proves it knows e without showing it. You send back Z, a proof you know z, and the exact Railgun note your dollars must go to.

  2. Ethereum

    The maker locks the dollars first

    The maker calls open(), putting the USDC into the contract under E, Z, two deadlines and your payout note.

  3. Your phone

    You check before you pay

    The app reads the swap back from Ethereum and only sends ZEC if every term matches.

  4. Zcash

    You send shielded ZEC

    It goes to the joint address. Neither side can spend it yet.

  5. Ethereum

    The maker confirms

    After 2 Zcash confirmations, the maker calls ready(), giving up its right to refund before t1.

  6. Ethereum

    You claim

    The app takes a claim lock, then calls claim(z). The contract checks z·G = Z and pays the USDC straight into your Railgun note. A relayer sends these transactions, so you never need ETH.

  7. Zcash

    The maker takes the ZEC

    Your half z is now public on Ethereum. The maker adds it to e and spends the ZEC. Both sides are paid.

03 Only two endings

Each one is fair to both sides.

z

Claimed

You reveal z

  • You get 0zkUSDC in your Railgun note.
  • The maker adds z to e and takes the ZEC.

The contract can only pay the note fixed at open.

e

Refunded

The maker reveals e

  • The maker gets its USDC back.
  • Your app adds e to your z and moves the ZEC back to you.

04 Why you don't have to trust anyone

The maker can't touch your ZEC without your half of the key. You only reveal that half by claiming, and the contract only pays the note you chose.

Deadlines

Two deadlines, t0 and t1, decide who may act, so a silent party can never trap the other.

Before the maker confirms

  1. From Open: The maker can refund.
  2. From t0: You can claim anyway. The maker can still refund.

After the maker confirms

  1. From Ready: Only you can act.
  2. From t1: You can still claim, and the maker can refund.

Locks

Before revealing anything, a side takes a lock (lockClaim or lockRefund). While one is held, the other side can't take one, so only one half is ever revealed. The app reveals z only once its lock has landed with at least 5 minutes to spare.

What if

The maker goes offline after you pay
You claim without it after t0.
You send too little
The maker refunds, which reveals e, so your ZEC comes back.
Your phone is off for hours
A confirmed swap waits until t1. Then the maker can only refund, which gives you the key back.
The relayer misbehaves
It can't redirect anything. The payout note is fixed, and every request is signed by your swap key.
A Railgun payout bounces
The funds return to a vault for that swap and can be shielded again.

05 The other way, dollars to ZEC

The same split key and contract, with the roles swapped.

  1. 01You lock 0zkUSDC straight from Railgun, with a refund note that only pays you.
  2. 02The maker sends ZEC to the joint address.
  3. 03Your app checks the ZEC is really there before you sign ready.
  4. 04The maker reveals its half to take the USDC. Your app adds it to yours and moves the ZEC into your wallet.

Until you sign ready, you can take your dollars back.

Compared with NEAR Intents

Two ways to swap ZEC for dollars.

Compared on NEAR Intents Veylor's bridge

Where NEAR Intents is ahead

It's fire-and-forget, while the bridge needs the app to come back online to claim. It also has deeper liquidity, takes any amount where the bridge uses fixed units, covers many chains and assets, and is a mature service in production.
