How it works

A split key and an atomic swap.

Your ZEC stays shielded, your dollars land in Railgun, and nobody, including us, ever holds your money.

01 The split key

Your ZEC goes to a fresh shielded address whose spend key is e + z. The maker holds e. You hold z, made fresh from your seed for each swap.

The Ethereum contract stores only the public points E = e·G and Z = z·G, on the Pallas curve, so it can check a half when one is revealed.

Taking money out of the contract means revealing your half, which hands the other side what it needs to take the ZEC.

02 A swap, ZEC to 0zkUSDC

  1. You and the maker

    Quote and accept

    The maker offers a price and proves it knows e without showing it. You send back Z, a proof you know z, and the exact Railgun note your dollars must go to.

  2. Ethereum

    The maker locks the dollars first

    Before you send anything, the maker calls open(), locking the USDC in the contract under E, Z, two deadlines and your payout note. The contract's source is open, and every swap is public on Etherscan.

  3. Your phone

    You check before you pay

    The app reads the swap back from Ethereum and only sends ZEC if every term matches.

  4. Zcash

    You send shielded ZEC

    It goes to the joint address. Neither side can spend it yet.

  5. Ethereum

    The maker confirms

    Once your ZEC has confirmed, the maker calls ready(), giving up its right to cancel. It can only do this before t0.

  6. Ethereum

    You claim

    The app hands your claim to a relayer, and you can close the app. The relayer calls claim(z) through Flashbots Protect, so z stays out of the public mempool until it lands. The contract checks z·G = Z, then payout() shields the USDC into your Railgun note, the only note it can pay. You never need ETH.

  7. Zcash

    The maker takes the ZEC

    Your half z is now public on Ethereum. The maker adds it to e and spends the ZEC. Both sides are paid.

03 Only two endings

Each one is fair to both sides.

z

Claimed

You reveal z

  • You get 0zkUSDC in your Railgun note.
  • The maker adds z to e and takes the ZEC.

The contract can only pay the note fixed at open.

e

Refunded

The maker reveals e

  • The maker gets its USDC back.
  • Your app adds e to your z and moves the ZEC back to you.

The maker can cancel before t0 if it never confirmed, or refund in the day after t1.

04 Why you don't have to trust anyone

The maker can't touch your ZEC without your half of the key. You only reveal that half by claiming, and the contract only pays the note you chose. With no owner or pause switch, nobody can change a swap once it opens.

Deadlines

Two deadlines, t0 and t1, cut every swap into windows, and in each only one side may act. A silent party can never trap the other.

If the maker never confirms

  1. From Open: The maker can cancel.
  2. From t0: You can claim.
  3. From t1: The maker can refund.
  4. From t1 + 24h: You can claim, for good.

Once the maker confirms

  1. From Ready: You can claim.
  2. From t1: The maker can refund.
  3. From t1 + 24h: You can claim, for good.

One side at a time

The clock and the swap's stage alone decide who may reveal, so only one half is ever revealed and no reveal can be raced. One held up still lands any time before its window closes, and your claim goes out only with at least 5 minutes of its window left.

What if

The maker goes offline after you pay
You claim without it from t0.
You send too little
The maker cancels, which reveals e, so your ZEC comes back.
Your phone is off for hours
Once the maker confirms, the relayer claims for you. If your phone went off before that, the swap waits for your claim until t1, then the maker refunds, which brings your ZEC back.
The relayer misbehaves
It can't redirect anything. The payout note is fixed, and every request is signed by your swap key.
A Railgun payout bounces
The funds return to a vault for that swap and can be shielded again.
