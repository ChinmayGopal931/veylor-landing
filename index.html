The first shielded bridge for Zcash

Swap ZEC for private dollars without ever unshielding.

Veylor is a self-custody Zcash wallet that swaps shielded ZEC for USDC held privately in Railgun on Ethereum, and back. It's an atomic swap: nobody holds your funds, and every failure ends in a refund.

The problem

Swapping through NEAR Intents leaves a public trail.

Zcash wallets such as Zodl swap through NEAR Intents. To trade ZEC there, you first send it out of the shielded pool to a transparent deposit address. That send stays public on the Zcash blockchain for good: the exact amount, the exact time, and a known NEAR Intents deposit address. It's the kind of transaction chain-analysis firms flag. Soon after, about the same value in USDC arrives from a solver on the other chain, so amount and timing link the two.

Veylor's bridge never unshields. Your ZEC goes to a fresh shielded address whose key is split between you and the other side of the trade, so anyone reading Zcash sees one ordinary shielded transaction.

Trustless, not trusted

An atomic swap. Nobody holds your money.

On the other side of every swap is a maker, which quotes a price and puts up the dollars. You don't have to trust it, or us. A contract on Ethereum enforces the deal, and the maker can't touch your ZEC without your half of the key.

Nobody holds your funds

Your ZEC waits at a shielded address that needs both halves of a split key, and the dollars wait in the contract. At no point does anyone, including us, hold your money.

No operator to trust

Through NEAR Intents, your ZEC sits in someone else's transparent address mid-swap, where it can be frozen, seized or lost. Here there's nothing to freeze. The maker can refuse to quote you, but it can't touch a swap already in flight.

Every failure ends in a refund

A swap has only two endings, each fair to both sides. Either you claim your dollars and the maker takes the ZEC, or the maker takes its dollars back and your ZEC comes back to you.

Dollars and ZEC, one wallet

Hold private dollars next to your ZEC.

Zcash wallets hold ZEC. Veylor also holds 0zkUSDC, which is USDC kept privately in Railgun on Ethereum, in the same wallet. Move into dollars to hedge, and back into ZEC when you choose, without unshielding.

The Railgun wallet is built in and out of sight. There's no second wallet to set up, no second seed to back up, and no ETH to buy for gas: a relayer sends the Ethereum transactions. You see a dollar balance next to your ZEC, with all your assets in one place.

How it works

A split key and an atomic swap.

Your ZEC never leaves the shielded pool, your dollars land straight in Railgun, and at no point does anyone, including us, hold your money.

01 The split key

Your ZEC goes to a fresh shielded Zcash address that nobody can spend alone. Its spend key is two halves added together. The maker keeps half e, and you keep half z, made fresh for every swap from your seed. Spending needs e + z.

The Ethereum contract stores only the public points E = e·G and Z = z·G, on the Pallas curve, so it can check a half when one is revealed.

The only way to take money out of the contract is to reveal your half of the key, and revealing it hands the other side what it needs to take the ZEC.

02 A swap, ZEC to 0zkUSDC

  1. You and the maker

    Quote and accept

    The maker offers a price and proves it knows e without showing it. You send back Z, a proof you know z, and the exact Railgun note your dollars must go to.

  2. Ethereum

    The maker locks the dollars first

    The maker calls open(), putting the USDC into the contract under E, Z, two deadlines and your payout note.

  3. Your phone

    You check before you pay

    The app reads the swap back from Ethereum and only sends ZEC if every term matches.

  4. Zcash

    You send shielded ZEC

    It goes to the joint address. Neither side can spend it yet.

  5. Ethereum

    The maker confirms

    After 2 Zcash confirmations, the maker calls ready(), giving up its right to refund before t1.

  6. Ethereum

    You claim

    The app takes a claim lock, then calls claim(z). The contract checks z·G = Z and pays the USDC straight into your Railgun note. A relayer sends these transactions, so you never need ETH.

  7. Zcash

    The maker takes the ZEC

    Your half z is now public on Ethereum. The maker adds it to e and spends the ZEC. Both sides are paid.

03 Only two endings

Each one is fair to both sides.

z

Claimed

You reveal z

  • You get 0zkUSDC in your Railgun note.
  • The maker adds z to e and takes the ZEC.

The contract can only pay the note fixed at open.

e

Refunded

The maker reveals e

  • The maker gets its USDC back.
  • Your app adds e to your z and moves the ZEC back to you.

04 Why you don't have to trust anyone

The maker can't touch your ZEC without your half of the key. You only reveal that half by claiming, and the contract only pays the note you chose.

Deadlines

Two deadlines, t0 and t1, decide who may act, so a silent party can never trap the other.

Before the maker confirms

  1. From Open: The maker can refund.
  2. From t0: You can claim anyway. The maker can still refund.

After the maker confirms

  1. From Ready: Only you can act.
  2. From t1: You can still claim, and the maker can refund.

Locks

Before revealing anything, a side takes a lock (lockClaim or lockRefund). While one is held, the other side can't take one, so only one half is ever revealed. The app reveals z only once its lock has landed with at least 5 minutes to spare.

What if

The maker goes offline after you pay
You claim without it after t0.
You send too little
The maker refunds, which reveals e, so your ZEC comes back.
Your phone is off for hours
A confirmed swap waits until t1. Then the maker can only refund, which gives you the key back.
The relayer misbehaves
It can't redirect anything. The payout note is fixed, and every request is signed by your swap key.
A Railgun payout bounces
The funds return to a vault for that swap and can be shielded again.

05 The other way, dollars to ZEC

The same split key and contract, with the roles swapped.

  1. 01You lock 0zkUSDC straight from Railgun, with a refund note that only pays you.
  2. 02The maker sends ZEC to the joint address.
  3. 03Your app checks the ZEC is really there before you sign ready.
  4. 04The maker reveals its half to take the USDC. Your app adds it to yours and moves the ZEC into your wallet.

Until you sign ready, you can take your dollars back.

Compared with NEAR Intents

Two ways to swap ZEC for dollars.

Veylor still offers NEAR Intents swaps for other assets. Between ZEC and dollars, it uses the bridge. Here is how the two routes compare.

NEAR Intents and Veylor's bridge, compared
Compared on NEAR Intents Veylor's bridge
What Zcash shows An unshield to a known NEAR deposit address, with the exact amount and time, public for good. One ordinary shielded transaction. No amount, no destination, no unshield.
What the dollar side shows USDC from a solver soon after, for about the same value. USDC paid into your Railgun note, inside Railgun's shielded pool.
Linking the two legs Amount and timing make the link. No shared hash, unlike hash-locked swaps, and nothing visible on the Zcash side.
Your funds mid-swap In someone else's transparent address, where they can be frozen, seized or lost. Held by nobody. There's nothing to freeze.
When something breaks A support ticket, and the refund may go to a transparent address. A refund, built in. At worst you wait until t1 for your ZEC to come back.
Censorship The solver or operator can block you. The maker can refuse to quote you, but can't touch a swap already in flight.
What the other side sees Your deposit, your payout address and your refund address. The same: the maker sees the swap it's party to. It can't see your other notes.

Where NEAR Intents is ahead

It's fire-and-forget, while the bridge needs the app to come back online to claim. It also has deeper liquidity, takes any amount where the bridge uses fixed units, covers many chains and assets, and is a mature service in production.

Powered by Railgun

Your dollars stay private in Railgun.

Railgun is an on-chain, non-custodial privacy system for Ethereum and other EVM chains. It uses zero-knowledge cryptography to keep tokens at private 0zk addresses, and to send, receive and use them privately. Its code has no owner, and there's no single official wallet: many independent wallets use it.

Veylor runs a Railgun wallet inside the app, so your 0zkUSDC sits in Railgun's shielded pool without a separate wallet or seed. Learn more at railgun.org.
